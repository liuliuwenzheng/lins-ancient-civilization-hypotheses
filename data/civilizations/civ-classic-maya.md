- (fact) UNESCO lists the Ancient Maya City of Calakmul (UNESCO 1061).
- (fact) Caracol research emphasizes long causeways and large-scale settlement integration (Chase & Chase).
- (fact) Chase excavation tradition discusses Caracol as a major Belize Classic polity (report tradition from 1987 on).
- (fact) Culbert’s Tikal ceramic reports supply ceramic benchmarks for Lowland Classic phasing (Tikal Reports tradition).
- (fact) Maya civilization is framed in continually revised English synthesis (Coe & Houston; synthesis orientation; no single-edition pages frozen).
- (fact) Copán’s Hieroglyphic Stairway is among the longest Classic Maya inscription monuments and a key dynastic source (Fash tradition; UNESCO 129).
- (fact) UNESCO lists the Maya Site of Copán (UNESCO 129).
- (fact) Epigraphy and archaeological synthesis frame the Classic Maya lowlands as a network of literate dynastic polities, with courts such as Tikal and Palenque among key centres (Martin & Grube 2008; UNESCO 64/411).
- (fact) Martin & Grube chronicle Classic lowlands as a multi-dynasty interactive political landscape (2008).
- (fact) Martin & Grube 2008 reconstruct Classic lowland dynastic political history from inscriptions (Chronicle of the Maya Kings and Queens).
- (fact) Mesoweb/FAMSI digital resources provide open orientation access to inscriptions and site reports (tier T).
- (fact) Classic Maya was not a single empire but multiple divine-kingship polities in coexistence and competition.
- (fact) Epigraphy and syntheses emphasize Classic lowlands as multi-polity competition rather than a single empire (Martin & Grube; Sharer & Traxler).
- (fact) Palenque inscriptions (including Temple of the Inscriptions tradition) support Pakal-dynasty reconstruction (Stuart and others).
- (fact) UNESCO lists Palenque for Classic architecture and inscriptions (UNESCO 411).
- (fact) The Petexbatun project supplies a regional case for Late Classic conflict and settlement change (Demarest).
- (fact) UNESCO lists Quiriguá Archaeological Park, noted for stelae and zoomorphs (UNESCO 149).
