## Claims (EN)

- (fact) UNESCO lists the Ancient Maya City of Calakmul (UNESCO 1061).
- (fact) Chase excavation tradition discusses Caracol as a major Belize Classic polity (report tradition from 1987 on).
- (fact) Coe & Houston provide a continually revised synthesis of Maya civilization.
- (fact) UNESCO lists the Maya Site of Copán (UNESCO 129).
- (fact) Epigraphy and archaeological synthesis frame the Classic Maya lowlands as a network of literate dynastic polities, with courts such as Tikal and Palenque among key centres (Martin & Grube 2008; UNESCO 64/411).
- (fact) Martin & Grube 2008 reconstruct Classic lowland dynastic political history from inscriptions (Chronicle of the Maya Kings and Queens).
- (fact) Epigraphy and syntheses emphasize Classic lowlands as multi-polity competition rather than a single empire (Martin & Grube; Sharer & Traxler).
- (fact) UNESCO lists Palenque for Classic architecture and inscriptions (UNESCO 411).
- (fact) Stelae and court inscriptions are core evidence types for reconstructing Classic dynastic history (Martin & Grube; Houston/Stuart/Taube orientation).
- (fact) Demarest and related traditions discuss diversity of Terminal Classic transformation and collapse models.
- (fact) UNESCO lists Tikal National Park for its Classic urban and temple complexes (UNESCO 64).
