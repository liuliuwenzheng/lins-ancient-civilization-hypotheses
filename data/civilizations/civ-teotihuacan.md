## Claims (EN)

- (fact) Research treats apartment compounds as the primary residential units and discusses multiethnic composition (Manzanilla; Cowgill).
- (fact) Isotopic and bioarchaeological studies discuss residential mobility and multi-origin populations (White/Price orientation).
- (fact) Cowgill 2015 provides a standard Anglophone synthesis entry on urbanism, phasing, and social networks.
- (hypothesis) Hypothesis: 6th–7th c. decline causes (internal conflict, climate, external enemies) remain debated and must be separated from stratified burn/abandonment evidence (Cowgill discussion context).
- (fact) Sugiyama and collaborators report large-scale sacrificial deposits associated with the Feathered Serpent Pyramid (Sugiyama tradition).
- (fact) INAH-coordinated excavation tradition has worked systematically along the Street of the Dead ritual axis and surrounding housing since mid-20th century (Cabrera Castro et al.).
- (fact) Art-historical syntheses discuss mural and sculptural programs as evidence for urban ideology (Pasztory orientation).
- (fact) Obsidian production and exchange studies place Teotihuacan in a regional obsidian economy (Spence).
- (fact) Mapping and synthesis frame Teotihuacan as a planned Classic metropolis in the Basin of Mexico, with ceremonial core and dense housing systematically surveyed (Millon TMP; Cowgill 2015; UNESCO 414).
- (fact) Millon’s Mapping Project systematically surveyed the city grid and surface collections, providing key evidence for urban scale (TMP; Antiquity data paper).
- (fact) UNESCO frames Teotihuacan as a planned Classic metropolis in the Basin of Mexico with monumental pyramids and the Temple of Quetzalcoatl (UNESCO 414).
