## Claims (EN)

- (fact) Burger's synthesis frames Chavín as a major Early Horizon Andean ceremonial and interaction centre (Burger 1992).
- (fact) UNESCO and scholarly syntheses frame Chavín de Huántar as a major Early Horizon Andean ceremonial and pilgrimage centre marked by distinctive lithic sculpture and monumental architecture (UNESCO 330; Burger 1992).
- (fact) Literature often compares Formative centres such as Kuntur Wasi and Pacopampa with the Chavín sphere without equating them as one polity (Burger / Nesbitt–Burger).
- (fact) Burger 1984 establishes a foundational field frame for prehistoric occupation and ceramic sequence at Chavín de Huántar (UCP Anthropology 14).
- (fact) Burger's ceramic sequence uses labels such as Janabarriu for Chavín floruit pottery (Burger 1984/1992); absolute dates remain revisable.
- (fact) Kembel/Rick research tradition discusses temple architectural phasing and spaces such as the circular plaza (architectural chronology orientation).
- (fact) UNESCO and syntheses frame the Lanzón and gallery system as hallmark elements of Chavín ceremonial architecture (UNESCO 330; Burger 1992).
- (fact) Nesbitt & Burger 2023 reconsider the Chavín phenomenon and interaction sphere in a twenty-first-century frame (Dumbarton Oaks / HUP).
- (fact) Stanford Rick long-term project continues excavation at Chavín de Huántar via galleries, plazas, and architectural-acoustics approaches (project portal / publication tradition).
- (fact) Chavín de Huántar architectural sculpture tradition includes tenon heads among other elements, as described by UNESCO and syntheses (UNESCO 330; Burger 1992).
- (fact) UNESCO lists Chavín de Huántar as World Heritage, emphasizing Formative Andean ceremonial architecture and lithic sculpture (UNESCO 330).
