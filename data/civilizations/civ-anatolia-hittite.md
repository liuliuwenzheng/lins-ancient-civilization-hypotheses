## Claims (EN)

- (fact) Syntheses discuss Alacahöyük early elite tombs and later Hittite-period remains as points in the central Anatolian sequence (Bryce; Collins).
- (hypothesis) Hypothesis/narrative: absolute dating and causes of terminal empire collapse (disaster, migration, internal politics) remain highly debated and must be separated from stratified evidence (Bryce narrative context).
- (fact) Scholarship and UNESCO frame Hattusa as the Hittite capital, with its tablet archives as core evidence for reconstructing the kingdom (Bryce 2005; UNESCO 377).
- (fact) UNESCO lists Hattusa as the Hittite capital World Heritage property, including components such as Yazılıkaya (UNESCO 377).
- (fact) Seeher and related work provide methodological entry points to Hattusa urban stratigraphy and absolute chronology (Seeher 2006; Neve/DAI tradition).
- (fact) Excavation tradition frames Kültepe-Kaneš as an Old Assyrian kārum tablet archive and Anatolian centre (Özgüç tradition).
- (fact) Regional landscape research discusses Hittite power within Bronze Age Anatolian landscapes and settlement networks (Glatz orientation).
- (fact) Excavation tradition frames Ortaköy-Şapinuwa as a Hittite provincial centre that yielded substantial tablet finds (Süel tradition; Bryce).
- (fact) Tablet archives and philological tradition are core evidence for reconstructing Hittite political history and ritual practice (Bryce; Beckman; van den Hout).
- (fact) DAI excavation tradition documents large-scale temple-district architecture as evidence for the capital’s ritual core (Neve tradition; UNESCO 377).
- (fact) UNESCO and syntheses frame Yazılıkaya as a rock sanctuary associated with Hattusa (UNESCO 377; Bryce 2005).
