- (fact) Beach and related historical work dialogue Shona oral/documentary traditions with archaeological stone capitals.
- (fact) Caton-Thompson’s 1931 work historiographically established African local construction against foreign-builder myths.

- (fact) Chirikure and recent archaeology refine metallurgy, dating and urban-economy discussions.

- (fact) Garlake’s classic monograph remains a foundational reference for Great Zimbabwe architecture and excavation history.

- (fact) The Hill Complex is commonly interpreted as an elite/ceremonial core sector.

- (fact) Huffman’s Iron Age handbook supplies a handbook entry to Zimbabwe Culture ceramics and settlement types.
- (fact) Zimbabwe Culture is placed in southern African Iron Age farming-society frames (Huffman 2007; models contested; handbook orientation).
- (fact) Khami and related successor stone capitals continue dry-stone tradition while shifting political centres.
