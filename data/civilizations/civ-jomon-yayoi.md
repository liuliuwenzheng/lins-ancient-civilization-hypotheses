## Claims (EN)

- (fact) Habu and others discuss variability in sedentism and social complexity under Jōmon hunter-gatherer economies (Habu Ancient Jomon).
- (fact) Kaner and related museum/synthesis orientations discuss contemporary interpretive frames for Jōmon material culture (Kaner).
- (fact) UNESCO component Odai Yamamoto is often included in discussions of early pottery in the Japanese archipelago (UNESCO 1632; research history).
- (fact) UNESCO component Ōyu Stone Circles represents a Late/Final Jōmon monumental stone-circle tradition (UNESCO 1632).
- (fact) Habu’s case study frames Sannai Maruyama as a large Jōmon settlement in a complex hunter-gatherer society and discusses growth and decline (Habu 2008 Antiquity).
- (fact) Research and UNESCO frame Sannai-Maruyama as a large northern Japanese Jōmon settlement within the Jōmon Prehistoric Sites serial property (Habu; UNESCO 1632).
- (fact) Sannai Maruyama is UNESCO serial component 1632-007 with an official site portal (UNESCO; jomon-japan.jp).
- (fact) UNESCO lists Jomon Prehistoric Sites in Northern Japan as a serial property including Sannai Maruyama, Ōyu Stone Circles, and other components (UNESCO 1632).
- (hypothesis) Hypothesis: relative weights of population replacement vs cultural transmission in Jōmon→Yayoi transition remain debated; separate from paddy/settlement evidence (Barnes/Mizoguchi discussion context).
- (fact) Research frames Yayoi as an agricultural tradition transition marked by wet-rice paddies (Barnes; paddy-field archaeology papers).
- (fact) Public and research orientation treats Yoshinogari as an illustrative large Yayoi moated settlement (park/Barnes orientation).
