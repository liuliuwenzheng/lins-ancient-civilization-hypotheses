## Claims (EN)

- (fact) von Dassow and related work discuss Alalakh as a city-society case under Mitanni imperial rule.
- (fact) The Amarna letter corpus preserves Mitanni diplomatic correspondence with Egypt and other powers (letter tradition; Liverani/Moran orientation).
- (fact) Bryce Kingdom of the Hittites and related syntheses discuss Mitanni–Hittite interaction.
- (fact) Historical and philological syntheses frame Mitanni as a Late Bronze Age Hurrian-led kingdom of Upper Mesopotamia–northern Syria, reconstructed largely from Hittite treaties and Amarna correspondence (Wilhelm 1989; Bryce 2005).
- (fact) The Kikkuli horse-training text survives via Hittite archives and is often linked to Mitanni technical traditions (textual P orientation).
- (fact) Diplomatic texts and treaty traditions (including Hittite-side) are key sources for Mitanni international relations (Letterman/Bryce orientation).
- (fact) Nuzi (Yorghan Tepe) tablet archives provide Hurrianized socio-legal and economic evidence (archive tradition).
- (fact) Pleiades and related gazetteers provide a place-record orientation for Nuzi.
- (fact) Tell Brak LBA levels provide field orientation for Upper Khabur Hurrian/Mitanni contexts (Oates tradition).
- (fact) Washukanni's capital location remains debated; Tell Fekheriye and others are candidate orientations, not settled identifications (Wilhelm; Bryce).
- (fact) Wilhelm 1989 and related syntheses place Mitanni in a Late Bronze Hurrian great-power frame.
