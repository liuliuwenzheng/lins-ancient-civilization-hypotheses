## Claims (EN)

- (fact) East St. Louis and American Bottom related clusters are discussed as interacting with the Cahokia centre (Pauketat 2009; Emerson 1997).
- (fact) Emerson 1997 discusses Cahokia's political landscape through archaeology of power and ideology (University of Alabama Press).
- (fact) Fowler's atlas establishes a foundational field frame for Cahokia mound numbering and excavation history (Fowler 1997).
- (fact) Milner 1998 synthesizes Cahokia Mississippian society archaeology in a chiefdom frame (Smithsonian).
- (fact) UNESCO and scholarly syntheses frame Cahokia Mounds as a major Mississippian ceremonial–urban centre in North America (UNESCO 198; Pauketat 2009).
- (fact) UNESCO and the atlas frame Monks Mound as among North America's largest prehistoric mounds and Cahokia's axial monument (UNESCO 198; Fowler 1997).
- (fact) Literature frames Mound 72 as one of Cahokia's famous Mississippian mortuary mounds (Fowler 1997; Pauketat 2004); details deferred to specialist reports.
- (fact) Pauketat's syntheses frame Cahokia as a major ceremonial–urban centre in the Mississippi Valley (Pauketat 2009/2004).
- (fact) American Bottom ceramic/phase labels (Lohmann, Stirling, Moorehead, etc.) describe Cahokia floruit and transition (Pauketat 2004); absolute dates remain revisable.
- (fact) UNESCO lists Cahokia Mounds State Historic Site as World Heritage, emphasizing Mississippian monumental mounds and urban planning (UNESCO 198).
- (fact) Cahokia Woodhenge/post-circles are discussed in syntheses as installations related to ceremonial calendrics (Pauketat 2009; Iseminger 2010).
