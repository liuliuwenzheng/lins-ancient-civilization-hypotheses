## Claims (EN)

- (fact) Blanton 1978 systematically mapped surface architecture and residential zones of the Monte Albán hilltop capital (Blanton 1978).
- (fact) Building J conquest slabs are discussed as epigraphic evidence of early-state expansion.
- (fact) Ceramic chronologies supply the ceramic framework for Monte Albán phasing (Winter orientation).
- (fact) Dainzú ballcourt reliefs extend Valley of Oaxaca ceremonial and ballgame imagery.
- (fact) Research links the Danzantes monument group with early Zapotec writing/political display (Urcid et al.; Marcus & Flannery).
- (fact) Danzantes are now widely interpreted as captive/sacrifice imagery rather than dancers (Urcid orientation).
- (fact) Early-state window focuses on Period I–II capital formation and expansion (Blanton; Spencer).
- (fact) Rosario/Tierras Largas window discusses Formative village sedentism and social complexity.
- (fact) Syntheses place Monte Albán’s foundation around ca. 500 BCE in the terminal Formative (Blanton; Marcus & Flannery).
- (fact) INAH manages Monte Albán as a federal archaeological zone and publishes materials (tier-T institutional).
- (fact) Late centres such as Lambityeco discuss valley reorganization after capital decline.
- (hypothesis) Mechanisms of Monte Albán Late Classic transformation/decline remain debated; retained as hypothesis.
- (fact) Marcus & Flannery 1996 synthesize how urban society evolved in the Valley of Oaxaca (Zapotec Civilization).
- (fact) Public/research orientation discusses Mitla as a later Zapotec centre (INAH/tier-T orientation).
- (fact) Mitla palace mosaic architecture represents a Postclassic Zapotec elite ceremonial centre.
- (fact) San José Mogote Formative centre supplies village–monument prehistory for Monte Albán’s rise.
- (fact) Settlement archaeology and UNESCO frame Monte Albán as the ancient Zapotec capital of the Valley of Oaxaca (Blanton 1978; UNESCO 415).
- (fact) Excavation tradition frames San José Mogote as a key Formative village centre before Monte Albán’s foundation (Flannery & Marcus).
- (hypothesis) Hypothesis/debate: mechanisms of Monte Albán expansion and ‘early state’ models (conquest, colonization, alliance) remain debated (Spencer/Redmond et al.).
- (fact) Valley survey frames Monte Albán as an early capital controlling the three-armed valley (Blanton and others).
- (fact) UNESCO lists Monte Albán as the ancient Zapotec archaeological site of the Valley of Oaxaca (with the historic centre of Oaxaca) (UNESCO 415).
- (fact) UNESCO list 415 inscribes Monte Albán and Oaxaca historic centre as World Heritage.
- (fact) Valley survey places Monte Albán within Oaxaca settlement hierarchy and hinterland networks (Blanton/Feinman/Kowalewski).
- (fact) Winter and related INAH excavation/survey traditions continue to fill stratigraphic detail at Monte Albán and in the valley (Winter tradition).
- (fact) Zapotec writing research discusses early Mesoamerican inscriptions recording places and personal names (Marcus).
- (fact) Zaachila Postclassic centre complements valley political geography beyond Mitla.
