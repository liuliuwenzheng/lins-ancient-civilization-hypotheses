- (fact) The Huari Ayacucho core remains the type site for Middle Horizon capital topography.
- (fact) Jennings and related work advance debates on Wari political organization, including beyond-empire models.
- (hypothesis) Jennings and peers debate whether Wari was a coercive empire, hegemonic network, or heterogeneous expansion.
- (fact) Menzel’s Middle Horizon ceramic seriation remains a foundational reference in Wari chronology debates.
- (fact) Menzel's stylistic seriation provides a classic frame for Middle Horizon Wari ceramic chronology (research history).
- (fact) Research frames Wari as a Middle Horizon complex society expanding from a central highland Ayacucho centre and establishing provincial administrative centres including near Cuzco (Schreiber 1992; Isbell & Schreiber 1978).
- (fact) Pataraya and related coastal-slope outposts orient Wari projection toward the Pacific slope (not a full provincial model).
- (fact) Pikillacta’s grid plan is a key case of Wari administrative architecture in the Cuzco region.

- (fact) Rectangular enclosure planning is a common morphological marker used to identify Wari provincial architecture.
- (fact) Syntheses often treat rectangular enclosures and orthogonal planning as architectural markers of Wari provincial centres (Schreiber; Isbell & McEwan).
- (fact) Wari is framed as a Middle Horizon expansive polity with provincial administrative features (Schreiber 1992; scholarly synthesis orientation; empire label not frozen as verdict).
