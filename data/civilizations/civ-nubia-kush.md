## Claims (EN)

- (fact) UNESCO lists Gebel Barkal and Napatan-region sites as a core Napatan religious–royal landscape (UNESCO 1073).
- (fact) The Oxford Handbook of Ancient Nubia provides a recent chapter-level entry to Kerma-through-Meroe archaeology and historiography (Emberling & Williams 2021).
- (fact) The Swiss excavation tradition frames Kerma as a Bronze Age centre with large mudbrick deffufa temple masses and related urban/cemetery layouts (Bonnet/Honegger; Edwards 2004).
- (fact) Syntheses frame Middle Nile Kush as a long sequence including Kerma and later Napatan–Meroitic phases (Edwards 2004; Welsby 1996).
- (fact) Edwards’s synthesis frames the Middle Nile as a long archaeological sequence including Kerma, Napatan, and Meroitic phases (Edwards 2004).
- (fact) UNESCO lists the Archaeological Sites of the Island of Meroe (including the town and pyramid cemeteries) as a key Meroitic property (UNESCO 1336).
- (fact) Research treats Meroitic writing as a principal textual archive medium for reconstructing Meroitic administrative and ritual practice (Rilly tradition; Török 1997).
- (fact) Syntheses and UNESCO frame Musawwarat es-Sufra as a large Meroitic temple complex (UNESCO 1336; Welsby 1996).
- (fact) Excavation memoir tradition treats cemeteries such as El-Kurru and Nuri as key evidence for Napatan royal burial sequences (Dunham MFA tradition; UNESCO 1073).
- (fact) Syntheses frame Naqa as a Meroitic temple and settlement centre (Welsby 1996; UNESCO 1336).
- (hypothesis) Hypothesis/narrative frame: the political role of Napatan rulers in Twenty-Fifth Dynasty Egyptian contexts must be read separately from cemetery–temple evidence (Welsby/Török).
