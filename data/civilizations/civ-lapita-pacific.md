## Claims (EN)

- (fact) Research links early Lapita appearance to Bismarck Archipelago production/exchange networks (Summerhayes; Kirch).
- (fact) Green and others define the Lapita Cultural Complex via dentate-stamped pottery and related assemblages (Green; Kirch).
- (fact) Research frames Lapita as an early Oceanic archaeological complex marked by dentate-stamped pottery, dispersing from the Bismarck Archipelago into Remote Oceania (Kirch; Green).
- (fact) Environmental archaeology discusses coastal/lagoon resource constraints on Lapita site choice (Nunn et al. orientation).
- (fact) Green’s retrospective (ANU OA) summarizes five decades of Lapita archaeological agendas and definitional change.
- (fact) The New Caledonia ‘Lapita’ locality is the name-giving source for dentate-stamped pottery (Sand; Green).
- (fact) Kirch’s synthesis frames Lapita peoples as archaeologically ancestral to the Oceanic world (Kirch 1997).
- (fact) Dating syntheses discuss rapid Lapita dispersal into Remote Oceania (Vanuatu, Fiji, Tonga, Samoa) (Sheppard; Kirch).
- (hypothesis) Hypothesis: reducing all Remote Oceanic settlement to a single ‘Lapita migration wave’ is too coarse; regional variation and multi-stage processes must be retained (Green/Sheppard discussion context).
- (fact) Kirch frames Talepakemalai as a key early Lapita site in Near Oceania (Mussau; Cotsen/UCLA publication).
- (fact) Bedford/Spriggs et al. frame Teouma as a key early Lapita cemetery/settlement in Vanuatu (Antiquity; excavation tradition).
