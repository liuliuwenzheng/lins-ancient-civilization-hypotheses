# 三星堆 / Sanxingdui

> **Status:** fact-library — factual summary only. Hypotheses belong in `hypotheses/`.
> **Depth:** follows [`docs/entry-depth.md`](../../docs/entry-depth.md). Every major section cites registered `src-*` ids.

## Summary

本条综述四川广汉三星堆青铜时代遗址的可核验事实。Li、Qiu、Guo 与 Li（2022）写明：遗址位于广汉县西部，成都东北约 40 公里；面积约 12 平方公里；年代约公元前 2700–1000 年；1929/1928 年前后发现，1934 年由华西协合大学博物馆 David C. Graham 主持首次发掘；1986 年发现一、二号祭祀坑，出土青铜、金、玉、象牙等文物九百余件（并引四川省文物考古研究所 1999《三星堆祭祀坑》）[`src-sanxingdui-li-qiu-guo-li-2022`][`src-sanxingdui-sichuan-jisikeng-1999`]。Bagley（1988）据早期发表材料概述：砖厂工人于 1986 年 7 月发现古玉后，考古队清理一号坑并随即发现更丰富的二号坑；一号坑呈长方形，约 4.6×3.5×1.6 米，出土三百余件器物（金、铜、玉、石、陶、海贝、13 根象牙及大量烧过的兽骨等），器物有焚烧痕迹，发掘者认为属一次祭祀后填埋；发掘者地层关系将两坑置于安阳期前半（约公元前 13–12 世纪）——标题中的「商时期」是**年代标签**，不是政体等同 [`src-sanxingdui-bagley-1988`]。Ge 与 Linduff（1990）向英语学界报告该遗址，并指出其发现挑战晚商前后「北中国青铜冶金唯一中心」叙事 [`src-sanxingdui-ge-linduff-1990`]。Sun Hua（2003）从地层与陶器类型学讨论三星堆遗址考古文化分期 [`src-sanxingdui-sun-hua-cultures-2003`]。Bagley 编（2001）《古代四川》与 Xu Jay「三星堆青铜」专章是英语物质文化综合入口 [`src-sanxingdui-bagley-ancient-sichuan-2001`][`src-sanxingdui-xu-bronze-2001`]。Flad（2012、2018）讨论四川盆地贵重物品与「城市作为技术」框架，并比较三星堆与金沙、殷墟 [`src-sanxingdui-flad-valuable-objects-2012`][`src-sanxingdui-flad-urbanism-2018`]。2020 年起祭祀区再发掘，新发现三至八号坑；四号坑六个碳十四年代为公元前 1260–924 校准年（Wu、Ran、Lei 2021；经 Li et al. 2022 转引）；作者工作方案将一至四、七、八号坑大致置于殷墟四期（约公元前 1200–1000 年），五、六号坑可能属西周早期（约公元前 1046–950 年）——文中亦承认祭祀坑绝对年代长期有争议 [`src-sanxingdui-li-qiu-guo-li-2022`][`src-sanxingdui-wu-ran-lei-2021`][`src-sanxingdui-zhao-nsr-2021`]。与中原商文化/商王国是否为同一政体，属开放研究问题，**本条不写成定论**。

This entry summarizes cited facts for the Sanxingdui Bronze Age site in Guanghan, Sichuan. Li, Qiu, Guo & Li (2022) state: the site lies in western Guanghan County, ~40 km north-east of Chengdu; covers ~12 km²; dates to c. 2700–1000 BC; was discovered around 1928/1929; first excavated in 1934 by David C. Graham; and in 1986 yielded Sacrificial Pits 1 and 2 with over 900 bronze, gold, jade and ivory artefacts (citing Sichuan Provincial Institute 1999) [`src-sanxingdui-li-qiu-guo-li-2022`][`src-sanxingdui-sichuan-jisikeng-1999`]. Bagley (1988): brickyard workers found archaic jades in July 1986; archaeologists excavated Pit 1 then a richer Pit 2; Pit 1 is rectangular ~4.6 × 3.5 × 1.6 m with more than 300 objects (gold, bronze, jade, stone, pottery, cowries, 13 elephant tusks, burnt animal bones), burned then ritually buried; excavators placed the pits in the first half of the Anyang period (13th or 12th century BCE) — “Shang period” in the title is a **chronological** label, not polity identity [`src-sanxingdui-bagley-1988`]. Ge & Linduff (1990) framed the finds as questioning north China’s primacy in late 2nd-millennium bronze metallurgy [`src-sanxingdui-ge-linduff-1990`]. Sun Hua (2003) defines archaeological cultures/phases at the site via stratigraphy and pottery typology [`src-sanxingdui-sun-hua-cultures-2003`]. Bagley ed. (2001) *Ancient Sichuan* and Xu’s “Bronze at Sanxingdui” chapter orient English material-culture synthesis [`src-sanxingdui-bagley-ancient-sichuan-2001`][`src-sanxingdui-xu-bronze-2001`]. Flad (2012, 2018) discusses valuable objects and urbanism-as-technology, comparing Sanxingdui with Jinsha and Yinxu [`src-sanxingdui-flad-valuable-objects-2012`][`src-sanxingdui-flad-urbanism-2018`]. Renewed excavation from 2020 revealed pits 3–8; six radiocarbon dates from Pit 4 give 1260–924 cal BC (Wu, Ran & Lei 2021, via Li et al. 2022); the authors’ working scheme places Pits 1–4, 7 and 8 roughly in Yinxu Phase IV (~1200–1000 BC) and Pits 5–6 probably in early Western Zhou (~1046–950 BC), while noting that pit chronology has long been debated [`src-sanxingdui-li-qiu-guo-li-2022`][`src-sanxingdui-wu-ran-lei-2021`][`src-sanxingdui-zhao-nsr-2021`]. Whether Sanxingdui was the same polity as the Shang kingdom remains **open research**.

## Geography & environment

三星堆位于四川盆地西北缘的成都平原，行政上属四川广汉；Li et al.（2022）给出相对成都约 40 公里东北、遗址面积约 12 km² 的定位 [`src-sanxingdui-li-qiu-guo-li-2022`]。Bagley（1988）亦写明广汉县、成都以北约 40 公里 [`src-sanxingdui-bagley-1988`]。成都平原是长江上游重要的冲积农业区；Flad & Chen（2013）将成都平原置于长江上游晚新石器至青铜时代结束的区域叙事中，强调政治、经济与礼仪中心不必空间重合 [`src-china-flad-chen-ancient-central-china-2013`]。盆地四周山地限制与长江上游通道，构成讨论区域互动时的地理背景——具体古道与「贸易路线」须另有出处，本条不编造 [`src-china-flad-chen-ancient-central-china-2013`][`src-sanxingdui-flad-valuable-objects-2012`]。教科文组织将三星堆与金沙、成都船棺墓群一并列入中国「古蜀国考古遗址」**预备名单**（Tentative List）——预备名单 ≠ 已列入世界遗产，且「古蜀国」为申报标题用语 [`src-sanxingdui-unesco-tentative-2013`]。

Sanxingdui sits on the Chengdu Plain at the northwestern edge of the Sichuan Basin, in Guanghan, Sichuan; Li et al. (2022) place it ~40 km north-east of Chengdu with an area of ~12 km² [`src-sanxingdui-li-qiu-guo-li-2022`]. Bagley (1988) likewise locates Guanghan Xian ~40 km north of Chengdu [`src-sanxingdui-bagley-1988`]. The Chengdu Plain is a major upper-Yangzi alluvial agricultural zone; Flad & Chen (2013) embed it in upper Yangzi Late Neolithic–Bronze Age regional narrative, noting that political, economic, and ritual centers need not coincide [`src-china-flad-chen-ancient-central-china-2013`]. Mountain rims and upper-Yangzi corridors frame interaction discussions — specific trade-route claims need dedicated sources [`src-china-flad-chen-ancient-central-china-2013`][`src-sanxingdui-flad-valuable-objects-2012`]. UNESCO lists Sanxingdui with Jinsha and Chengdu boat-coffin tombs on China’s **Tentative List** for “Archaeological Sites of the Ancient Shu State” — tentative ≠ inscribed, and “Ancient Shu State” is nomination wording [`src-sanxingdui-unesco-tentative-2013`].

## Key sites

- **三星堆遗址本体 / Sanxingdui site** — 约 12 km² 的大型中心；含城墙/聚落与祭祀区等遗存类型（细节随发掘阶段公布）[`src-sanxingdui-li-qiu-guo-li-2022`][`src-sanxingdui-ge-linduff-1990`]。
- **1986 一、二号祭祀坑 / Sacrificial Pits 1–2** — 正式报告：四川省文物考古研究所 1999《三星堆祭祀坑》（P）[`src-sanxingdui-sichuan-jisikeng-1999`]。Bagley（1988）英译概述一号坑尺寸、器物门类与焚烧—填埋解释 [`src-sanxingdui-bagley-1988`]。Li et al.（2022）转引两坑青铜、金、玉、象牙等九百余件 [`src-sanxingdui-li-qiu-guo-li-2022`]。
- **2020 起三至八号坑 / Pits 3–8** — 祭祀区再发掘新发现；Li et al.（2022）Project Gallery 与 Zhao（2021）NSR 亮点报道研究史节点；定量器物总账以正式中文简报/报告为准，本条不编造件数 [`src-sanxingdui-li-qiu-guo-li-2022`][`src-sanxingdui-zhao-nsr-2021`]。
- **四号坑 / Pit 4** — Wu et al.（2021）给出 AMS 与贝叶斯校准；平面近方形，边长约 2.9 米，最深约 1.6 米（中文报告）[`src-sanxingdui-wu-ran-lei-2021`]。
- **金沙（对照） / Jinsha (comparative)** — Flad（2012、2018）将金沙与三星堆并置讨论贵重物品与城市技术；教科文预备名单亦将二者归入同一申报标题——**并置 ≠ 已证明的简单「迁都」叙事** [`src-sanxingdui-flad-valuable-objects-2012`][`src-sanxingdui-flad-urbanism-2018`][`src-sanxingdui-unesco-tentative-2013`]。

- **Sanxingdui site** — large center (~12 km²) with settlement/walls and sacrificial-area remains as published [`src-sanxingdui-li-qiu-guo-li-2022`][`src-sanxingdui-ge-linduff-1990`].
- **1986 Sacrificial Pits 1–2** — primary monograph Sichuan 1999 [`src-sanxingdui-sichuan-jisikeng-1999`]; Bagley (1988) English summary of Pit 1 dimensions, object classes, burn-and-bury interpretation [`src-sanxingdui-bagley-1988`]; Li et al. (2022) cite >900 bronze/gold/jade/ivory artefacts [`src-sanxingdui-li-qiu-guo-li-2022`].
- **Pits 3–8 (from 2020)** — Li et al. (2022) and Zhao (2021) mark the research-history node; full object tallies await formal Chinese reports — none invented here [`src-sanxingdui-li-qiu-guo-li-2022`][`src-sanxingdui-zhao-nsr-2021`].
- **Pit 4** — Wu et al. (2021) AMS/Bayesian dates; roughly square ~2.9 m side, deepest ~1.6 m [`src-sanxingdui-wu-ran-lei-2021`].
- **Jinsha (comparative)** — Flad (2012, 2018) juxtaposes Jinsha with Sanxingdui on valuables and urbanism; UNESCO tentative list groups them — **juxtaposition ≠ proven simple capital relocation** [`src-sanxingdui-flad-valuable-objects-2012`][`src-sanxingdui-flad-urbanism-2018`][`src-sanxingdui-unesco-tentative-2013`].

## Urbanism, architecture & institutions

三星堆被英语与汉语学界讨论为成都平原青铜时代的大型礼仪—政治中心。Ge & Linduff（1990）强调其作为西南青铜时代遗址的重要性 [`src-sanxingdui-ge-linduff-1990`]。Flad（2018）将「城市」视为技术/实践组合，用规模、分化、中心性与展演等维度比较三星堆、金沙与殷墟——**这是分析模型，不是对「城市/国家」法律定义的最终判决** [`src-sanxingdui-flad-urbanism-2018`]。祭祀坑本身是高度仪式化的埋藏设施：Bagley（1988）转述发掘者对一号坑「一次祭祀后焚烧填埋」的解释；坑壁未见火烧痕迹，故火焚发生在入坑之前 [`src-sanxingdui-bagley-1988`]。城墙、宫殿式建筑与居址的平面细节应以正式发掘报告为准；本条在未登记专报页码前不编造墙长或宫殿编号 [`src-sanxingdui-li-qiu-guo-li-2022`][`src-sanxingdui-sichuan-jisikeng-1999`]。

Sanxingdui is discussed as a major ritual–political center of the Chengdu Plain Bronze Age. Ge & Linduff (1990) stress its importance as a southwestern Bronze Age site [`src-sanxingdui-ge-linduff-1990`]. Flad (2018) treats urbanism as a technological assemblage, comparing Sanxingdui, Jinsha, and Yinxu along scale, differentiation, centrality, and performance — an **analytical model, not a final legal definition of “city/state”** [`src-sanxingdui-flad-urbanism-2018`]. The sacrificial pits are highly ritualized deposits: Bagley (1988) relays the excavators’ single-ceremony burn-then-bury interpretation for Pit 1; pit walls showed no fire traces, so burning occurred before deposition [`src-sanxingdui-bagley-1988`]. Wall and palace plans require formal reports — no invented wall lengths or palace IDs [`src-sanxingdui-li-qiu-guo-li-2022`][`src-sanxingdui-sichuan-jisikeng-1999`].

## Writing, language & administration

与晚商安阳甲骨文传统不同，三星堆目前**没有**可与甲骨文比肩的、已系统释读的本遗址文字档案。坑内器物组合以青铜雕像/面具、神树、金器、玉器、象牙等礼仪美术为主，而非泥板或甲骨行政文书 [`src-sanxingdui-bagley-1988`][`src-sanxingdui-xu-bronze-2001`][`src-sanxingdui-li-qiu-guo-li-2022`]。个别符号、纹饰或「文字线索」的报道若出现，须单独登记出处并标明释读争议；本条不把未破译符号写成已识读行政文字。行政组织形态（王权、神权、城邦等）属于解释层面，留待有出处的假说条目——事实栏只记物质与坑藏证据类型。

Unlike Late Shang Anyang oracle bones, Sanxingdui currently **lacks** a systematically readable local writing archive comparable to jiaguwen. Pit assemblages center on bronze figures/masks, trees, gold, jade, and ivory ritual arts rather than tablet or oracle-bone administration [`src-sanxingdui-bagley-1988`][`src-sanxingdui-xu-bronze-2001`][`src-sanxingdui-li-qiu-guo-li-2022`]. Any claimed graphs or “script clues” require separate registered sources and explicit undeciphered status; this entry does not treat undeciphered marks as readable administration. Polity form (kingship, theocracy, city-state, etc.) is interpretive — keep in sourced hypotheses; fact columns record material and pit-deposit evidence types.

## Economy, subsistence & craft

成都平原农业与四川盆地资源（含金属原料获取的区域讨论）构成三星堆社会的生计背景；Flad & Chen（2013）提供长江上游区域综合入口，但本条不在未核对植物考古表格前给出主粮比例 [`src-china-flad-chen-ancient-central-china-2013`]。礼仪经济方面，Flad（2012）讨论青铜、玉、金、象牙等贵重物品在三星堆与金沙的生产、使用与仪式性弃置 [`src-sanxingdui-flad-valuable-objects-2012`]。青铜铸造是关键手工业：Xu（2001）与 Bagley 编（2001）目录系统介绍雕像、面具、神树等器类与造型传统 [`src-sanxingdui-xu-bronze-2001`][`src-sanxingdui-bagley-ancient-sichuan-2001`]。坑内海贝、象牙等提示远程或区域性物资流通的可能性——**流通机制（朝贡/贸易/战争俘获等）未在本条定为单一模型** [`src-sanxingdui-bagley-1988`][`src-sanxingdui-flad-valuable-objects-2012`]。

Chengdu Plain agriculture and Sichuan Basin resources (including regional metal-procurement discussions) frame Sanxingdui subsistence; Flad & Chen (2013) orient upper Yangzi regional synthesis, but no staple percentages are asserted without archaeobotanical tables [`src-china-flad-chen-ancient-central-china-2013`]. On ritual economy, Flad (2012) examines production, use, and ritual discard of bronze, jade, gold, and ivory at Sanxingdui and Jinsha [`src-sanxingdui-flad-valuable-objects-2012`]. Bronze casting is central craft: Xu (2001) and Bagley ed. (2001) survey figures, masks, trees, and related forms [`src-sanxingdui-xu-bronze-2001`][`src-sanxingdui-bagley-ancient-sichuan-2001`]. Cowries and ivory in pits suggest long-distance or regional material flows — **mechanism (tribute/trade/booty, etc.) is not fixed to one model here** [`src-sanxingdui-bagley-1988`][`src-sanxingdui-flad-valuable-objects-2012`].

## Notes

- **Sanxingdui ↔ Shang (open research — not settled fact):** Bagley’s 1988 title uses “Shang period” as a **chronological** label tied to Anyang-period dating of the pits; Ge & Linduff (1990) and later work use Sanxingdui to argue for multi-centred Bronze Age development. None of that equals a verified claim that Sanxingdui was a Shang royal centre or “the same kingdom.” Keep polity-identity hypotheses in `hypotheses/` [`src-sanxingdui-bagley-1988`][`src-sanxingdui-ge-linduff-1990`][`src-sanxingdui-li-qiu-guo-li-2022`].
- **Chronology debate:** Li et al. (2022) explicitly note that sacrificial-pit chronology has long been debated. Pit 4’s 1260–924 cal BC range and the Yinxu IV / early Western Zhou working scheme are **author-stated research positions**, not library dogma. Recalibrations and full formal reports may shift endpoints [`src-sanxingdui-li-qiu-guo-li-2022`][`src-sanxingdui-wu-ran-lei-2021`].
- **UNESCO Tentative List:** ID 5816 is not an inscribed World Heritage property; “Ancient Shu State” is nomination language [`src-sanxingdui-unesco-tentative-2013`].
- **Jinsha succession models:** Flad’s comparative framework and heritage grouping do not by themselves prove a single-state capital move; treat succession narratives as open research.
- **No forged images:** do not invent or upload fake artefact photographs; cite museum/excavation publications when illustrating later.
