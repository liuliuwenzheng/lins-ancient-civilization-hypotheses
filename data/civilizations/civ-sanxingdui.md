# 三星堆 / Sanxingdui

> **Status:** library-seed — cited factual overview. Hypotheses belong in `hypotheses/`.

## Summary

本条概述四川广汉三星堆青铜时代遗址的可引用种子事实。Li、Qiu、Guo 与 Li（2022）在 *Antiquity* 中写明：遗址位于广汉县西部，成都东北约 40 公里；面积约 12 平方公里；年代约公元前 2700–1000 年；1928 年发现，1934 年由华西协合大学博物馆 David C. Graham 主持首次发掘；1986 年发现一、二号祭祀坑，出土青铜、金、玉、象牙等文物九百余件（并引四川省文物考古研究所 1999《三星堆祭祀坑》） [`src-sanxingdui-li-qiu-guo-li-2022`][`src-sanxingdui-sichuan-jisikeng-1999`]。Bagley（1988）据早期发表材料概述：砖厂工人于 1986 年 7 月发现古玉后，考古队清理一号坑并随即发现更丰富的二号坑；一号坑呈长方形，约 4.6×3.5×1.6 米，出土三百余件器物（金、铜、玉、石、陶、海贝、13 根象牙及大量烧过的兽骨等），器物有焚烧痕迹，发掘者认为属一次祭祀后填埋；发掘者地层关系将两坑置于安阳期前半（约公元前 13–12 世纪） [`src-sanxingdui-bagley-1988`]。Ge 与 Linduff（1990）向英语学界报告该遗址，并指出其发现挑战晚商前后「北中国青铜冶金唯一中心」叙事 [`src-sanxingdui-ge-linduff-1990`]。2020 年起祭祀区再发掘，新发现三至八号坑；四号坑已发表六个碳十四年代为公元前 1260–924 校准年；作者工作方案将一至四、七、八号坑大致置于殷墟四期（约公元前 1200–1000 年），五、六号坑可能属西周早期（约公元前 1046–950 年）——文中亦承认祭祀坑绝对年代长期有争议 [`src-sanxingdui-li-qiu-guo-li-2022`]。与中原商文化/商王国是否为同一政体，属开放研究问题，**本条不写成定论**。

This seed summarizes cited facts for the Sanxingdui Bronze Age site in Guanghan, Sichuan. Li, Qiu, Guo & Li (2022) state: the site lies in western Guanghan County, ~40 km north-east of Chengdu; covers ~12 km²; dates to c. 2700–1000 BC; was discovered in 1928; first excavated in 1934 by David C. Graham; and in 1986 yielded Sacrificial Pits 1 and 2 with over 900 bronze, gold, jade and ivory artefacts (citing Sichuan Provincial Institute 1999) [`src-sanxingdui-li-qiu-guo-li-2022`][`src-sanxingdui-sichuan-jisikeng-1999`]. Bagley (1988), summarizing early Chinese reports: brickyard workers found archaic jades in July 1986; archaeologists excavated Pit 1 and then a richer Pit 2; Pit 1 is rectangular ~4.6 × 3.5 × 1.6 m with more than 300 objects (gold, bronze, jade, stone, pottery, cowries, 13 elephant tusks, burnt animal bones), burned then ritually buried; excavators placed the pits in the first half of the Anyang period (13th or 12th century BCE) [`src-sanxingdui-bagley-1988`]. Ge & Linduff (1990) reported Sanxingdui to Anglophone archaeology and framed the finds as questioning north China’s primacy in late 2nd-millennium bronze metallurgy [`src-sanxingdui-ge-linduff-1990`]. Renewed excavation of the sacrificial area from 2020 revealed six new pits (3–8); six radiocarbon dates from Pit 4 give 1260–924 cal BC; the authors’ working scheme places Pits 1–4, 7 and 8 roughly in Yinxu Phase IV (~1200–1000 BC) and Pits 5–6 probably in early Western Zhou (~1046–950 BC), while noting that pit chronology has long been debated [`src-sanxingdui-li-qiu-guo-li-2022`]. Whether Sanxingdui was the same polity as the Shang kingdom remains **open research**, not a settled fact in this library.
