## Claims (EN)

- (fact) Excavation tradition identifies Tall-e Malyan as highland Anshan known from texts (Sumner tradition; Potts 2016).
- (fact) Regional archaeology places Chogha Mish in Susiana prehistoric to Proto-Elamite settlement and ceramic sequences (Potts 2016; Carter & Stolper 1984).
- (fact) Political-history syntheses often summarize a lowland–highland dual-centre structure via titles such as ‘king of Susa and Anshan’ (Carter & Stolper 1984; Potts 2016).
- (fact) Excavation and synthesis link Haft Tepe with Middle Elamite Kabnak (Negahban; Potts 2016).
- (fact) Handbooks such as The Elamite World place Elam in a SW Iranian–Mesopotamian interaction frame (Álvarez-Mon et al. 2018).
- (fact) Syntheses place Neo-Elamite in a continuous SW Iranian sequence preceding Achaemenid ascendancy (Potts 2016).
- (hypothesis) Hypothesis frame: Proto-Elamite tablets are often discussed as an early administrative writing system, but language affiliation and scope remain debated (Potts synthesis context).
- (fact) Susa Acropolis/Ville Royale memoir traditions supply the main published framework for stratified architecture and ceramics (Steve–Gasche MDAI tradition).
- (fact) UNESCO frames Susa as mounds with continuous superimposed urban settlement from the late 5th millennium BCE, including administrative, residential, and palatial remains (UNESCO 1455).
- (fact) Scholarly syntheses frame Elam as an ancient political–cultural entity of southwestern Iran centered on regions including Susiana and Anshan (Potts 2016; Carter & Stolper 1984).
- (fact) UNESCO and syntheses frame Tchogha Zanbil (Dur-Untash) as a Middle Elamite ziggurat and sacred precinct (UNESCO 113; Potts 2016).
