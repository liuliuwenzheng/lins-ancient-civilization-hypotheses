## Claims (EN)

- (fact) Akkadian letters and treaty texts place Ugarit in the LBA international correspondence network (Lackenbacher/Malbran; Singer).
- (fact) Corpus tradition treats Ugaritic alphabetic-cuneiform tablets as a core archive for NW Semitic and ritual texts (Pardee; KTU/CAT; Bordreuil & Pardee).
- (fact) Archive-topography studies discuss relations between tablet findspots and urban functional zones (van Soldt; Yon).
- (fact) Excavation records a terminal LBA destruction horizon; absolute dating and external-cause explanations remain debated (Yon 2006).
- (fact) The Handbook of Ugaritic Studies provides a multidisciplinary entry to language, archaeology, and history (Watson & Wyatt 1999).
- (fact) Geographic syntheses discuss coastal–inland territorial structure of the kingdom of Ugarit (Saadé; Yon).
- (fact) Excavation tradition links Minet el-Beida with Ugarit’s port function (Yon; Schaeffer tradition).
- (fact) Excavation and synthesis identify Tell Ras Shamra as ancient Ugarit (Yon 2006; Schaeffer tradition).
- (fact) Schaeffer’s Mission de Ras Shamra from 1929 established the founding field tradition for Ugarit (CRAI notices; Yon synthesis).
- (hypothesis) Hypothesis: single-cause ‘Sea Peoples’ models for Ugarit’s destruction are underdetermined and should remain hypotheses, not facts (Yon/Singer discussion context).
- (fact) Yon’s synthesis, based on long-term excavation, frames Ras Shamra as an LBA capital with palace, acropolis temples, and port links (Yon 2006).
