## Claims (EN)

- (fact) Classical texts (Periplus tradition) and archaeology identify Adulis as a Red Sea trade port (Burstein; Manzo).
- (fact) Syntheses discuss Adulis as a Red Sea port linked to Aksum (Munro-Hay; Phillipson).
- (fact) British Institute in Eastern Africa and related projects continue to publish Aksum urban and funerary archaeology.
- (fact) British Institute in Eastern Africa / Munro-Hay excavation traditions continue Aksum fieldwork (P orientation).
- (fact) Royal Christianization and church landscapes are a core turning point in Aksumite history from the 4th century (Heldman; Munro-Hay).
- (fact) Aksumite coinage supplies epigraphic–iconographic evidence for royal names, religious symbols, and Red Sea economy.
- (fact) Aksumite coinage tradition is treated in numismatic catalogues and Munro-Hay syntheses (catalogue orientation; no forged accession numbers).
- (fact) The Deutsche Aksum-Expedition (Littmann et al.) founded modern stelae and inscription documentation (P-tradition).
- (fact) Bieta Giyorgis excavations supply a key modern dataset for Aksumite elite residence and chronology (Fattovich & Bard).
- (fact) Finneran’s Archaeology of Ethiopia places Aksum within a longer Ethiopian archaeological sequence (2007).
- (fact) UNESCO and scholarly syntheses frame Aksum as a major highland kingdom of the 1st–8th centuries CE in northern Ethiopia, marked by stelae, royal tombs, and Red Sea trade (UNESCO 15; Phillipson 2012).
- (hypothesis) Late Aksumite transformation correlates with Red Sea trade shifts; specific causality remains discussed.
- (fact) The Deutsche Aksum-Expedition (Littmann et al.) forms an early scientific recording tradition.
- (fact) Matara, Qohaito and related Eritrean highland sites extend the geography of the Aksumite world.
- (fact) Met Heilbrunn Timeline provides a public-facing Kingdom of Aksum orientation essay (tier T).

- (fact) Red Sea trade linked Aksum to late Roman/Byzantine and Indian Ocean networks (Manzo; Munro-Hay).
- (fact) Aksum’s giant stelae are core material evidence of an African late antique monumental tradition (Littmann DAE; UNESCO 15).
- (fact) Giant stelae are a core monumental category in UNESCO and synthetic descriptions of Aksum (UNESCO 15; Phillipson).
- (fact) UNESCO Aksum documents page aggregates World Heritage protection materials.
